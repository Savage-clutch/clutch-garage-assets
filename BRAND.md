## Rules that always apply
- Copy: no BS. Short, one idea per line, verb-first headlines and CTAs. Sentence case. No exclamation points,
  em dashes, all caps or emoji. Canadian spelling (colour, centre, grey, licence; -ize endings).
- Words: car not vehicle, used not pre-owned, Clutch Certified car, 210-point inspection, trade-in.
- "That's Clutch" only after a proven fact, once, last, no punctuation, never next to bad news.
- Clutch Red (#FF464C) is the accent for energy and never marks a problem. Plum (#460B28) for
  confidence and proof, Lavender (#F1EDF8) for calm and stressful moments, Soft Black (#191919) for text. Flat colour, no
  gradients. Circular Bold or Medium headlines, Poppins as fallback.
- Pages and slides sit on white or a Garage colour field (Plum for proof, Lavender for calm, Red for a hero slide
  or physical work). Never a black or near-black background, and never follow a device's dark mode.
- Never invent colours, lockups, icons, Clutch Moments, prices, stats or policies. The live Garage page wins.

# The Garage Philosophy
_Live from the Garage https://garage.clutch.ca/document/1#/brand/the-garage-philosophy. Last modified: 2026-10-22T17:19:00_

The Garage philosophy guides how we design, build, and show up as Clutch. It's the mindset behind every experience, asset, and interaction, and the playbook for how we look, how we sound, and how we make people feel at every touchpoint.
At its heart are three principles: Energy, Confidence, and Charm/Calm. Together, they shape everything we create.

#### These three pillars guide everything we create: from our tone of voice, 
the designs we craft, to how we deliver brand experiences.

### Energy
Energy means bringing the excitement. Buying a car is a big moment, and our brand should feel just as exciting. That energy should show up everywhere, from our 3D icons to the product UI and every customer conversation. Any time someone interacts with Clutch, it should feel upbeat, optimistic, and full of momentum.

### Confidence
Confidence means showing we know what we're doing. Every car gets a 210-point inspection from our mechanics before it earns the Clutch Certified badge, and the process behind it is rigorous. That same care should come through in our brand. Whether we're on a billboard, on TV, in an ad, an email, or the product itself. When we're talking about what we've done or what's coming next, we should sound clear, assured, and composed.

### Charm/Calm
Charm/Calm means bringing humanity and small moments of delight. Even when we're serious and focused, we make room for warmth. That could be a playful line of copy, a Clutch Moment icon, or a small detail that makes someone smile. Whatever form it takes, it should remind people that there are real humans behind Clutch: friendly, thoughtful, and calm.

### Contextual Placements

Where our work appears is a big part of our philosophy. A good line 
in the wrong place is just another ad. The same line in the right
place feels like it was written for the person reading it.

Before we write a headline, we ask where it will live, who will see it there, 
and what they're thinking about in that moment. Then we let the setting
do some of the work.

Before anything ships, check the setting as carefully as the headline.
If the placement doesn't add to the message, look for a better spot.

# Voice and content

> **Not on the Garage yet.** Draft guidance the server serves until the brand team publishes a Garage page called "Voice and content". When that page exists, it replaces this automatically. Where this conflicts with a live Garage page, the page wins.

## 1. Rule zero: no BS

Every word earns its keep. Say it once and say it short. If the sentence works without the clause, cut the
clause. Never stack reassurances: one said plainly beats three said carefully.

| Fluff | Clutch |
|---|---|
| "Not feeling it? You've got 10 days or 750 km to return your car, full refund, no explanations owed. Here's how it works." | "Not feeling it? You've got 10 days to love it or return it, no questions asked." |

Cut list: qualifiers ("likely", "anytime", "simply", "just"), policies restated after the reader already
trusts them, cute add-ons riding on a finished sentence, trailing reassurance ("Walk away. No pressure." says
no pressure twice), and any second clause that is the first clause in a sweater.

## 2. Succinct or complete: pick the job

| Moving someone: be succinct | Proving something: be complete |
|---|---|
| Headlines, CTAs, notifications, social, OOH | Certified, pricing, policies, how it works, inspection reports |
| One idea, one line, verb first | Every fact that earns trust, in sentences that stay short |
| "Get a real offer in minutes" | "Every car is inspected by our own Clutch mechanics. Brakes, suspension, engine, body. Nothing is skipped." |

The tell is where length comes from. Length from more facts is clarity. Length from longer sentences is fluff.
When we're earning trust, detail is the swagger. Never cut facts to seem punchy.

## 3. The four voice pillars

The voice is the Garage Philosophy said in words. Forward-thinking and Empowering carry Energy and Confidence;
Human and Cheeky are Charm/Calm doing its job in the writing.

**Forward-thinking.** Talk about the smarter way, not the old way. Name what we fixed for people, not the
feature that fixed it. Plain words for smart things. One contrast with the old way is plenty.
"Skip the lot, keep your weekend." / "Selling your car? Get paid in about 300 seconds."

**Empowering.** The customer is the hero, we're the crew. Make the reader the subject ("You've got options",
not "We offer options"). Lead with the verb when you want action. End with a step they can take, no dead ends.
Back confidence with proof (a number, a guarantee, a report), never adjectives. Confidence, never pressure:
no countdown timers, no fake urgency.

**Human.** Direct, warm, never condescending. Contractions on. Buzzwords and legalese off. A car is the
second largest purchase most people make: take the moment seriously without making it heavy.
Test: would you say it to someone standing in their driveway?

**Cheeky.** Deadpan, never wacky. The joke never announces itself; the reader smirks after they already got
the message. Mechanics: double meanings ("You might be sitting on a small fortune"), the turn ("Selling your
car is a big deal. So get the best deal."), absurd specificity ("paid in about 300 seconds"), a gentle jab at
the old way ("No haggle. No hassle. No sketchy meetups."). If the reader has to solve the joke to find the
offer, cut the joke. Cheeky lives in headlines, social and campaigns, appears as a pinch in product, and
never appears in Steady.

## 4. Registers: Steady, Everyday, Hype

Our voice never changes. Our register does, because it's how we read the room. Registers mirror the colours.

| Register | When | How | Visual pairing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Steady | Bad news, money stress, returns, low offers, delays | Calm, plain, zero hype, nothing decorative. The situation in sentence one, the next step by sentence two. Never chirpy, never corporate. | Lavender, Plum. Never a big red moment. |
| Everyday | The default: browsing, updates, scheduling, most product and email | The four pillars as written. Helpful first, clever second. | Full system, red as accent |
| Hype | Launches, giveaways, NBA moments, milestones | Short and punchy, energy from the words. Hype a real thing, not an adjective. Use it like Clutch Red: sparingly. | Red fields, hero type |

Same message, three volumes. Aim for the middle column:

| Context | Not enough | Just right | Too far |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trade-in offer ready | "Your vehicle valuation is now complete." | "Your offer: $8,450. Good for 7 days." | "CHA-CHING 💰 Your ride = serious $$$" |
| Explaining Certified | "All vehicles undergo a comprehensive 210-point inspection process." | "210 checkpoints. 40% don't make it. Yours did." | "The most INSANE inspection known to man." |
| Delivery day | "Your delivery is scheduled for tomorrow." | "Tomorrow. 10 to 12. Your car's coming." | "OMG TOMORROW!!! 🎉🚗🔥" |
| Financing approved | "Your financing application has been approved." | "You're approved. Here's your rate and what's next." | "WOOHOO! Time to make it rain! 🤑" |
| Low offer (Steady) | "Unfortunately your vehicle did not meet valuation criteria." | "Your offer: $6,800. Not what you hoped? See the breakdown, ask for a second look, or walk away." | "Still a great deal! 🙌" |

Dealership speak is always "too far". The industry we replaced is the voice we never borrow.

## 5. "That's Clutch": the verbal logo

It does in words what the Big C does in design, so it gets logo rules:
- **Proof first.** Only after a demonstrated fact. "210 checkpoints. That's Clutch" works. "Amazing service. That's Clutch" doesn't.
- **Once per piece, always last.** Nothing follows it.
- **No punctuation when standalone.** Never "That's Clutch." or "That's Clutch!" In running prose it punctuates normally.
- **Never modified.** No "That's so Clutch", no "Pretty Clutch", no line break between the words.
- **Everyday and Hype only.** Never in Steady.
- **Always capital C.**

| Write | Never write | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Clutch Certified car | CPO, certified pre-owned, a Certified vehicle | In full on first mention and in headlines, then "the car" or "your car". Certified is a proof point, not a brand: no own colours, voice or identity. |
| used car | pre-owned, second-hand, previously enjoyed | We say used, proudly. "A used car that made the cut." |
| 210-point inspection | rigorous / comprehensive / multi-point inspection | Always the number. Companion stat: about 40% make the cut. |
| car | vehicle | "Vehicle" only in legal, insurance and inspection-report copy. |
| trade-in | trade in, tradein | The product term for offers, appraisals and pickup. "Sell your car" is campaign language only. |
| Clutch | clutch, CLUTCH | Capital C in copy, always. Lowercase `clutch` / `clutch.ca` is the wordmark, not a spelling. |
| the Big C | the C logo, logo icon | The 3D symbol. Not the same thing as the emblem. |
| the flatbed | the tow truck, car hauler | "The Clutch flatbed truck" on first formal mention. |
| Clutch Moments | Clutch moments, 3D icons (in customer copy) | Plural, capitalized as a pair. |
| no middlemen | no middle-men, no middlemens | Use where the old way is the contrast. |

Banned outright: blowout, sale event, everything must go, hassle-free, seamless, best-in-class, world-class,

| centre, theatre, litre, kilometre, metre (unit) | center, theater, liter, kilometer |
| grey | gray |
| cheque (payment) | check |

## 8. Mechanics

- **Case.** Sentence case everywhere: headlines, buttons, nav, subject lines. Never all caps (Garage: Typography).
- **Periods follow grammar, never emphasis.** A complete-sentence headline takes one ("We'd never sell you this
  car."). A fragment doesn't ("Skip the lot, keep your weekend"). UI labels and buttons don't. Standalone
  "That's Clutch" never does. Never add punctuation for emphasis (Garage: Typography).
- **Exclamation points: never.** Any register, any channel. If a line needs one to feel exciting, the line isn't exciting.
- **No em dashes.** Use a comma, colon or period. (En dashes only for number ranges in tables: 10–12. In copy, write "10 to 12".)
- **Emoji: approximately zero.** Default is none, even on social. Never in transactional or Steady copy.
- **Contractions: always on.** "You're approved", never "You are approved".
- **Numbers.** Numerals when the number is the point: 210 checkpoints, 10 days, 750 km, 40%. Spell out casual
  numbers in prose ("one visit", "a couple of days").
- **Money.** $8,450. Comma separator, no decimals unless cents matter. No "$8.4K" outside social and chart axis labels ($39K).
- **Dates and times.** "Thursday, Aug 21". Conversational: "between 10 and 12". Precise: "10 a.m. to 12 p.m."
- **Verb-first CTAs.** "Get my offer", "See the breakdown", "Book a test drive". Never "Click here", "Submit", "Learn more" alone.
- **Headline + subhead.** The subhead adds context, never repeats the headline (Garage: Typography).
- **Language.** English only for now (Ontario first). FR-CA is reserved; brand names never translate.

## 9. Things we never do

1. Exclamation points, ALL CAPS, or punctuation for emphasis.
2. Em dashes.
3. Dealership speak (blowout, sale event, act now) or corporate filler (seamless, solutions, leverage).
4. Fake urgency, countdown pressure, or anything that pushes instead of empowers.
5. Adjectives where a number should be ("rigorous" instead of 210).
6. Stacked reassurances or restating the policy twice.
7. Hype or jokes in a Steady moment. Never "That's Clutch" next to bad news.
8. Red, hype or celebration attached to problems, warnings or errors (Clutch Red always means positive momentum).
9. "Pre-owned", "vehicle", "CPO", or treating Clutch Certified as its own brand.
10. Inventing facts, prices, stats, policies or promises. Every claim must be real and current.
11. Locking up a headline, offer or body copy with the logo (Garage: Typography).
12. Writing a line without knowing where it will live (Garage: Contextual Placements).

## 10. How much to say

Reference: https://www.clutch.ca/value-tracker. It says everything a person needs in about 150 visible words.

- **One idea per block: a heading and one line.** "Priced on real sales / 5 million+ data points, including what
  cars sold for." No second paragraph.
- **Headings of 2 to 5 words do the work.** The support line adds a fact or a reason, never restates the heading.
- **Numbers are headings when the number is the point.** "1.6 million cars tracked", not "We track a lot of cars".
- **Show what they need to decide. Hide the rest behind a tap.** Method, caveats and edge cases go in an
  expander, a FAQ or a "View all". Never on the main surface.
- **Steps are verb-first.** "Tell us about your car", "Get your value report", "Choose how often we check in",
  "Decide when the time is right". Each with one line: what they do, how long it takes.
- **Lists stop at four.** Four facts, four steps, five FAQs. If there are more, there's a "View all".
- **Labels are 1 to 3 words.** "Adds value", "Lowers value", "Today", "Current value".
- **Say the time cost when it's small.** "It takes about two minutes."
- **End with control.** "Keep it or bring it to Clutch, with your value in hand either way."
- **Cut before you add.** If you're about to write a third line, first try deleting the second.

What the reference page gets wrong, so don't copy it: "vehicle" (write car), Title Case headings
("Frequently Asked Questions" → "Frequently asked questions"), full-sentence support lines without periods.

## 11. Review process

Copy is reviewed in this order. Steps 1 to 5 are on the writer (person or AI). Steps 6 and 7 are other people.

1. **Brief check.** Before writing, answer: Where will this live? Who sees it there and what are they thinking
   in that moment? Which register (Steady, Everyday, Hype)? Is the job to move or to prove?
2. **Write long, then cut.** Apply rule zero. Remove one clause and reread. If it still works, it was fluff.
3. **Mechanical check.** Run `check_copy` (MCP tool) or `scripts/check_copy.py` (skill). It catches spelling,
   banned words, exclamation points, em dashes, emoji, all caps, word-list errors and "That's Clutch" misuse.
   Fix everything it flags or note why it's an exception.
4. **Voice check.** The ship checklist below. Read it aloud.
5. **In-placement check.** Look at the line where it will actually run: in the layout, on the slide, in the
   email client, on the billboard mockup. "Check where the work will appear as carefully as you checked the
   headline" (Garage: Layout / Composition, Before it ships).
6. **Fact check by the owner.** Any price, rate, stat, timeline, guarantee or policy is confirmed by the team
   that owns it (ops, finance, legal) before it ships. Proof points (210, about 40%, 10 days) must be current.
7. **Brand sign-off.** New taglines, campaign lines, anything with "That's Clutch" in Hype, partner and
   co-branded copy, and anything going on the Garage go to the brand team.

### Ship checklist
- [ ] Does every word earn its keep?
- [ ] Forward-thinking: does it name what's better, in plain words?
- [ ] Empowering: does the reader leave with an obvious next step that's theirs?
- [ ] Human: would you say it aloud in someone's driveway?
- [ ] Is the wink in the right place, and the message still first?
- [ ] Right job: succinct when moving, complete when proving?
- [ ] Right register for the moment, not just the message?
- [ ] Clutch's words: used not pre-owned, car not vehicle, 210 not "rigorous", trade-in hyphenated?
- [ ] Canadian spelling, sentence case, no exclamation points, no em dashes, no emoji?
- [ ] Checked in its real placement?

### When an AI writes Clutch copy
- Give 2 to 3 options, recommend one, and say which register and job you wrote for.
- Run `check_copy` on every option before showing it.
- Never invent a price, stat, policy or promise. Use a placeholder like [PRICE] and say it needs the owner's number.
- If the brief is a Steady moment, say so and write it Steady even if asked for "fun".

## 1. Setup [Applied]
- Canvas 1920 × 1080 (16:9). Margins about 6% (roughly 115px sides, 65px top and bottom).
- Font: Circular. In Google Slides or anywhere Circular isn't installed, Poppins (Regular, Medium, SemiBold).
  Export to PDF with fonts embedded when sending outside Clutch. Never let a slide fall back to a system font:
  load the actual font files and check every weight renders.
- Weight carries the hierarchy, so it is never one weight throughout:

  | Role | Circular | Poppins fallback |
  |---|---|---|
  | Headline, hero headline | Bold | SemiBold (600, the heaviest Poppins allowed) |
  | Subheader, lead | Medium | Medium (500) |
  | Body, notes | Regular | Regular (400) |

  A title slide is a Bold headline over a Medium subheader at roughly half its size, never two Regular lines.
- Type on slides, from the Garage scale: hero headline 160–200 Bold (title and statement slides), headline
  90–110 Bold, subheader 40 Medium, lead 28 Medium, body 20–28 Regular, source notes 14. Never below 14.
- Colour fields are flat. No gradients, no textures, no drop shadows.
- Before building, check Good Stuff (wallpapers) and any existing Clutch deck template.

## 4. Rules [Applied from Garage]
- **One idea per slide, one hero per slide.** If a slide needs two heroes, it's two slides.
- **The headline is the message.** Write the takeaway, not a topic label: "40% of cars don't make the cut", not
  "Inspection results". Sentence case, verb first where it moves someone. All copy rules apply (see the copy guide).
- **Red is the accent.** In a deck of 20 slides, full red fields are 1 to 3 (open, close, one hype moment). Red
  never marks a problem, risk or decline; use Plum or Soft Black for those.
- **Logo.** The cover follows the reference below (white wordmark, top left, about 260px). Closing slides match
  the cover. Content slides carry the wordmark in the same corner on every slide at a consistent smaller size,
  never below 100px wide, colour per the background table. The 1/5-of-diagonal size is for ads, posters and
  other single layouts, not deck covers.
- **Don't use all the colours at once.** A deck can move through red, plum and lavender sections, but each slide
  uses one field plus Soft Black or White type.
- **Charts.** Flat bars and lines, no 3D, no gradients. Highlight one series in Clutch Red or Dark Cherry, the
  rest in Plum tints or grey. Label directly instead of a legend where possible. Numbers in Soft Black or White.
- **Imagery.** Real Clutch photography from the Media Kit. No generic stock, no golden-hour filters, no generated
  people without Creative Director approval.
- **Icons.** No icon grids or bullet icons. If a slide needs an icon, use the 2D set; if it needs a hero visual,
  use one Clutch Moment.
- **Bullets.** Three at most, each a short line. Long proof goes in an appendix slide (complete, not cramped).
- **Motion.** None, or a simple cut or fade. No bounces, spins or fly-ins.
- **Space.** "Space is what makes the type look sure of itself." Resist filling empty areas.

Start from the `get_web_tokens` output (generated from the live Garage palette). It has the Garage palette as tokens, the CircularXX → Poppins stack,

### Web rules
From the Garage:
- **Backgrounds are white.** White is the Garage's default for content-heavy pages and product UI. Never build a
  Clutch page on black or near-black: black isn't a Clutch colour field. When a section needs weight, use Plum.
  Put `<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">` in the head and don't follow the device's dark mode.
- White is the default background for content-heavy pages and product UI. Lavender only for stressful content
  (pricing, forms, paperwork), never as a blanket background.
- Red for CTAs, badges, the one spot the eye lands first. Never for errors or warnings. Use Soft Black text with
  a plain icon, or Dark Cherry, for validation states.
- Soft Black (#191919) for text and line work, not pure black.
- Logo: `clutch` wordmark, red on light, at least 100px wide in the header. Emblem only for favicon and app icon.

- 2D icon set for UI; one Clutch Moment as a section hero where a journey moment is shown.
- Left-aligned body text. Generous whitespace. One hero per view.

From shipped builds [Web baseline]:
- Exactly one accent colour. No gradient heroes, no glassmorphism, no glow rings on inputs.

## Reference build: Value Tracker

https://www.clutch.ca/value-tracker is the best live example of the system working on screen: hierarchy,
how much to show, motion, Clutch Moments, 2D icons and colour meaning. When in doubt on a web or product
surface, do what this page does. (Observed Oct 2026, desktop 1440 and mobile 390.)

### Page anatomy: one job per section
1. **Hero.** Headline (fragment, no period), one support line, the action (two fields + one red button), then
   the product itself as the image: a real car riding on the value line with one number on it ($38,350).
2. **The proof, interactive.** "See what your car's worth today" + one support line + a 3M / 6M / 12M
   segmented control + the chart. Nothing else.
3. **Why trust it.** One Clutch Moment (a red scale weighing car keys) over "Accuracy backed by data", then four
   facts, each a bold line plus one muted line. The fourth fact is a number used as a heading: "1.6 million cars tracked".
4. **What's coming.** "Know your milestones": a chart with three numbered markers, each a date plus a 3 to 5 word event.
5. **What moves it.** Filter (All / Adds value / Lowers value) + a carousel of cards: label, title and a +/−
   button. Detail opens on tap, it isn't printed on the card.
6. **How it works.** Four numbered steps, each a verb-first title plus one line.
7. **FAQs.** Five questions, collapsed. "Ask anything else" field, "View all FAQs" button.

### Hierarchy
- Two levels per block: a bold heading and one muted support line. Never a third paragraph.
- Section heading left, support line or control right (desktop); stacked on mobile.
- Big type is for headlines and the one number that matters. Everything else is body size.
- One number is the hero of the page (the value), repeated in the hero, the chart tooltip and the milestone
  chart. It appears in a black pill with white type wherever it's live.
- Whitespace separates sections; thin 1px rules separate items inside a section. Light grey (#F9F9F9-ish)
  bands alternate with white to pace the scroll. No heavy cards around text.

### How much to show
- **Show** the number, where it's going, and the next action. **Hide** the method, the caveats and the detail
  behind a disclosure: card "+" buttons, collapsed FAQs, "View all FAQs".
- Every fact gets a heading of 2 to 5 words and at most one line of support.
- Lists stop at four items (facts, steps, cards on screen). More goes behind "View all" or a carousel.
- Charts carry three or four gridlines, short axis labels ($39K, Jul, Today) and one labelled point. No legend
  when the colours explain themselves.
- Numbered markers (1, 2, 3) link a chart to its labels instead of repeating text on the chart.

### Colour carries meaning
- Clutch Red only for the primary action, the step numbers and the Clutch touch on the Moment.
- **Good news on Plum, bad news on Lavender.** "Adds value" cards are Plum with white type; "Lowers value" cards
  are Lavender with Soft Black type. Negative change is never red. This is the Garage colour logic in product:
  Confidence for proof, Charm/Calm for the stressful part.
- Data in Dark Cherry and Plum tints: the history line solid, the forecast as a two-step tinted band.

### Clutch Moments and 2D icons on one page
- **One Clutch Moment per page,** as the hero of the trust section, not as decoration on every section. It
  echoes the page's own data: the scale's readout shows the value. The Clutch touch is the red housing.
- **Animated Moments move only the part that moves.** The scale stays still; only the display counts up and
  settles on the number (GIF, about 5s, loops). No spinning, bouncing or camera moves.
- **2D icons are functional only:** +, −, chevrons, close, phone. One stroke weight, monochrome, inside a small
  circular button. Never decorative, never next to every heading.

### Motion spec (from the page's CSS)
| What | Motion | Timing |
|---|---|---|
| Hero value line | Draws left to right (stroke-dashoffset) with a soft fade-in | 2800ms, cubic-bezier(0.55, 0.05, 0.15, 1) |
| Value marker | Lands after the line: scale 0.4 → 1, fade in | 550ms, 2200ms delay, cubic-bezier(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1) |
| Value card | Rises 14px and fades in with the marker | 600ms, 2200ms delay, same curve |
| Chart replot (3M/6M/12M) | Fade in, rise 6px | 400ms ease-out |
| Segmented control thumb | Slides (left + width) | 400ms ease-in-out |
| Carousel | Translate | 550ms, cubic-bezier(0.4, 0, 0.2, 1) |
| Hover, borders, colour | Opacity / colour / border | 200ms ease-in-out |

Principles: motion tells the data story once (line draws, then the number lands), then stops. Ease-out curves,
no overshoot, no springs. Interface transitions stay at about 200ms. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`: show the
final state with no draw.

### Don't copy these from the page
- "what your vehicle is worth": the word list says "car".
- Title Case headings and nav ("Frequently Asked Questions", "Sign In", "Browse Popular Models"): sentence case.
- Support lines that are full sentences but end without a period ("It takes about two minutes"): follow the
  period rule consistently.
- Small labels above titles ("Adds value", "01"): fine as category tags inside product cards and steps, but
  don't turn them into eyebrows over section headings.

## Product UI: buttons, colour by section, icons (reference: a clutch.ca car page)

Observed on https://www.clutch.ca/vehicles/129614 (Oct 2026) alongside Value Tracker. For anything that looks

### Buttons
Flat pills, always. **No gradients**, no shadows, no outlines on filled buttons. Hover is an opacity shift.

|---|---|---|
| Primary | Clutch Red (#FF464C) pill, white text, SemiBold 16px, 48px tall; full width inside a purchase card | The one main action in a view ("Start purchase"). Never two red buttons in one view. |

Labels are verb-first and sentence case ("Start purchase", "Get pre-qualified"). The token CSS has these as
`.btn-primary`, `.btn-secondary`, `.btn-on-plum`, `.btn-tertiary`.

### Colour by section
- **White** is the page. Sections are separated by thin #EEEEEE rules and space, not by colour bands.
- **Cards** are white with a 1px #EEEEEE border and about 16px radius (the purchase card, feature cards).
- **Red** appears only on the primary button, progress markers (the "Next steps" dots) and the Clutch touch in
  a Clutch Moment or the flatbed. Nothing else on the page is red.
- **Plum** marks proof and the good stuff: the inspection card is a Plum field with white text, the Clutch
  Certified badge and a white pill button; the "Free" badge is a Plum pill; the recommended plan ("Most popular")
  gets a Plum header bar and a Plum outline around its column.
- **Neutral grey** (#EEEEEE) badges carry costs or neutral facts ("$129"). A cost is never Plum or Red.
- **Lavender** shows up where the moment is reassurance: the 10-day return Clutch Moment sits on a Lavender cushion.
- **Soft Black** for headings, values and secondary buttons; body text a softer dark grey; labels lighter grey.
- **Green** check marks appear for verified facts and included items (0 accidents, warranty included). Green is
  not a Garage colour: use it only for that status check, small, never as a fill, heading or button.

### Icons
- **2D icons** (the Garage set, thin line, monochrome) label data: a spec row is icon + bold value + muted label
  ("Automatic AWD / Transmission"), and feature cards open with one small icon. Same size and stroke everywhere.
- **Clutch Moments** (3D) head a section, one per section and never in a list: the flatbed over delivery
  ("Get it as early as tomorrow"), the car key on a lavender cushion over the 10-day return, a key with a red
  calculator next to financing ("Ready to finance?"). Centred above a centred heading, or beside a heading + CTA.
- Partner and data-source logos (Carfax, the car maker) appear small and only where that source is cited.

### Information pattern
- Section heading on the left, "View all N" link on the right.
- Value first, label second: "8.8 city 7.5 highway" over "Litres / 100km".
- Show three, hide the rest behind "+ N more" or a carousel.
- Numbers that matter get their own big moment (the rating "4.3"), everything else stays body size.

### Don't copy from that page
"Better than a test drive - try it for 10 days" and "no impact on your credit score" use a hyphen as a dash
(use a colon or full stop); "Next Steps" and "MOST POPULAR" break sentence case; "But don't worry, we have
options to keep you protected!" has an exclamation point; "This vehicle has..." says vehicle, not car.

## Where the sources disagree

1. **Red type on dark.** The Garage approves red type on Soft Black and Plum fields (slides, print, OOH). Shipped
   web builds rejected red text on black backgrounds. Follow the Garage for slides and print; follow the web rule on screens.
2. **Eyebrows.** The Garage type scale lists an Eyebrow style. Web builds don't use them. Slides may; web doesn't.
3. **Dark backgrounds (resolved).** Brand pages are white; black is not a Clutch colour field. Plum carries weight

4. **Input shadows.** Value Tracker's hero fields use a soft shadow; the web baseline says borders only. Inside
   clutch.ca product, match the product. In standalone builds (tools, microsites), use borders.

#### Circular is our primary font
Circular is a geometric sans serif that feels clean, modern, 
and approachable. It's easy to read at any size and flexible
enough for both small interface text and bold campaign
headlines.

#### Headline Styles 
Headlines set the tone, so keep them strong and simple. 
Use Circular in bold or medium only. These weights give 
headlines enough presence on their own, with no need
for extra styling.

#### Headline Styling
Headlines work best when they're simple and precise.
Set them for clarity first, then character. A few small choices 
in spacing, weight, and punctuation keep every headline 
feeling like Clutch, on a product screen or a billboard.

Line spacing

Auto works for most headlines. At very large sizes, tighten it to about 90 percent. For body copy, open it up to about 120 percent if the lines start to feel cramped.

Letter Spacing

Start at about −20, or −2 percent, depending on your software. Then adjust by eye and fix the spacing between any letter pairs that look too tight or too loose.

Punctuation

Use periods only where the grammar needs them.
A full-sentence headline gets one, a fragment doesn't. Never add punctuation just for emphasis.

#### Body copy styles
Body copy does most of the explaining, so it needs to be easy to
read, whether someone skims it or reads it in full. Use a clear 
hierarchy of sizes and give body text a little extra line spacing
for accessibility, so it feels open and comfortable to read.

1. Headline, Bold. 2. Supporting copy, Regular.

1. Body, Regular, centred.  2. Line length, kept short.

#### Type Hierarchy
Clarity comes first. Use size and weight to build a clear hierarchy 
that makes every line easy to read. Exact sizes will vary by format,

type to suit the space.

- Logo
The Clutch Hero logo. 
Always the primary brand sign-off and the anchor for any layout.

- Heading
Circular XX Bold. 
The first thing read, and often the only thing read.

- Subheading
Circular XX Medium. 
Roughly half the size of the heading. 
Adds context, never repeats it.

#### Type Colour
Type should always be easy to read and fit with the rest of the palette. 
Use Soft Black for most headlines and body copy, white on dark 
backgrounds, and Clutch Red sparingly for short callouts. If you're
unsure about legibility, choose the option with the strongest contrast.

When Circular isn't available, use Poppins. Stick to Regular, Medium, 
or Semi-Bold to stay close to the Clutch look. Only use it in edge
cases, like system-generated content or emails that can't load
custom fonts.

# Composition
_Live from the Garage https://garage.clutch.ca/document/1#/layout/composition. Last modified: 2026-10-08T14:22:00_

#### Equally balanced layouts
Start with the content, then choose the layout. 
Each core layout is built from the centre out, so type, imagery, and logo each have a clear job and feel connected.

Let your copy, your visuals, and where the piece will appear guide which layout you pick.

#### 60/40
Use this layout when the copy should lead. Give about 60 percent of the space to type and 40 percent to imagery, with the logo large and clear in its own corner. Keep the split clean, and only add gutters if the content needs more room.

#### Copy-First
When the headline is strong enough to stand on its own, let it. This layout works best for outdoor ads. Skip the image and let the headline carry the message, with the logo in the bottom right. A subhead can sit below it, but it's optional.

#### 30/40/30
Use this for portrait formats where the logo should stand out without competing with the message or visuals. Give about 30 percent of the space to copy, 40 percent to imagery, and 30 percent to a clear logo band.

### When to use which
Start with the format, then think about what the piece needs to do. Portrait or landscape, digital or outdoor, each suits a different layout. For content-heavy pieces, choose the simplest split and leave enough space in the margins and gutters so nothing feels cramped.

#### 60/40 in use
Use 60/40 when the copy comes first. 
It works well for longer lines, busier images, or when you want a strong block of brand colour that's still easy to read.

#### Copy-First in use
Use this layout when the line does all the work. It's best for bold, simple messages that need to land fast, especially in outdoor ads or anywhere you want nothing to distract from the words.

#### 30/40/30 in use
Use this layout when copy, imagery, and logo each need their own space. It works best in portrait formats, with the logo in its own band so the message, the visual, and the brand each get noticed without crowding each other.

### Before it ships 
Check where the work will appear as carefully as you checked the headline. 
The setting should support the message, not work against it.

#### Handy tips
Layouts give every piece structure and hierarchy, and they also shape how someone reads it.

Keep body copy left-aligned. It gives the eye a clean starting edge, makes scanning easier, and helps headlines, subheads, and details all feel like part of the same system.

Use generous margins and whitespace around type and imagery. Space is what makes a layout feel confident, not crowded, and it gives each element room to do its job.

Every layout needs one clear focal point, whether it is a line of copy, an image, or the logo. Decide what you want people to notice first, then let everything else support that choice instead of competing with it.

# Clutch Moments
_Live from the Garage https://garage.clutch.ca/document/1#/iconography/clutch-moments. Last modified: 2026-10-22T17:19:00_

A Clutch Moment is a small moment of delight in the customer journey, like the 210-point inspection or having 10 days to love your 
car or return it.

Our 3D icons turn these moments into physical objects, lit like studio photos. Each one captures the instant before something happens: a button about to be pressed, a key about to turn, a light about to change.

### Clutch Moment Principles 
- Real, not rendered.
Every Moment should look like an object you could reach out and touch. Use hard edges, honest materials, and soft, controlled lighting on a neutral background to keep it believable.

- Something Clutch on every one.
Each Moment needs a clear Clutch touch, usually a primary colour (most often red) on the part that moves. 
Sometimes it's a small charming detail instead. It's never missing.

- Materials carry the philosophy. 
Brushed aluminum and polished metal show Energy. Matte white and concrete show Confidence. 
 Warm wood shows Charm/Calm. Choose the material that matches the feeling of the moment.

- The object is the idea. 
Never illustrate an abstract concept directly. Pick an object people recognize instantly, like a shot clock for urgency, a traffic light for go, or a smart lock for trust, and build it in the Clutch palette.

- Caught just before it happens. 
Show each object mid-action: a switch mid-flip, a key half-turned, a dial at max. 
They're still frames that feel like something is about to happen.

Questions to ask before reaching for one:
- Is this a moment or a function? 
Clutch Moments mark moments of delight in the journey. For everyday actions and UI, use a 2D icon.

- Can people tell what it is right away? 
If someone has to stop and figure it out, it's the wrong object.

- Where's the Clutch touch? 
You should be able to point to it, whether it's a primary colour or a small charming detail. If you can't, it isn't finished.

- Is it already in the library?
If it isn't, ask the brand team to create it. Don't build your own.

#### Real, not rendered

Every icon should feel like a real, engineered object, something you could pick up off a table. 

- Lighting: Shoot it like a studio product photo, with a neutral background, a soft controlled shadow, and a single key light.

- Shape: Use detailed geometry with real bevelled edges. No clay looks, sculpted forms, or soft edges.

- Materials: Every material should look true. Metal should feel like metal, wood grain should run the right way, and concrete should have weight.

Clutch's physical spaces are warm, functional, and thoughtfully designed, with a Scandinavian feel. The icons use the same kinds of materials.

Wood is an optional accent, used only in small touches when it adds warmth or meaning, and it shouldn't appear in every icon.

Frosted and translucent acrylic are only for icons used in digital settings, like app screens and interface elements. Everything else should be solid, physical, and real.

#### The Facility Material Language

White and neutral grey set the scene. Red is never decoration, it's the signal. It marks the action: the button being pressed, the caliper gripping, the cord carrying power. Use one touch of Clutch Red per icon, and make it count.

- Background: White (#FFFFFF) with a neutral grey backdrop (#E8E8E8 to #F0F0F0 range).

- Signal: Clutch Red (#FF464C), on the single active or key element only

- Sparingly: Plum (#460b28) or Lavender (#F1EDF8)  for shadows and inside surfaces.

- Never more than one red element in the same icon.
